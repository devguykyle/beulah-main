Beulah and Main is a publication about recovery without costume.

It is interested in public theology, household formation, American memory, city pleasures, old books, practical counsel, and the kind of literary imagination that can put a whole family near the fireplace at the end of a long day.

The first issue is seeded with work and archival leads from the Haynes Institute, including material that once lived near the Heritage American project. Beulah and Main is larger and less on the nose by design: not an institute, not a heritage costume, and not a blog diary, but an editorial front porch for discovered work old and new.

The house style is serious but warm: newspaper bones, fairway air, bright city windows, good coffee, good beer, and old books that have not stopped speaking.
