One of the sharper pieces in the starting archive asks an old biblical question in a modern register: "Should you help the wicked and love those who hate the Lord?"

The essay begins with Jehoshaphat's alliance with Ahab and treats the story as a warning about disordered affection. Shared blood, shared history, shared enemies, and shared national memory can all become arguments for unfaithful alliance.

Beulah and Main is not trying to soften the archive into pleasant memory. Some recovered pieces should unsettle the reader. This one belongs in Public Square because it presses doctrine, kinship, political temptation, and pastoral responsibility into one demanding question.
