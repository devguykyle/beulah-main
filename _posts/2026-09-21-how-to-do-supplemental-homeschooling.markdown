Not every family can change its entire schooling arrangement at once. But many families can reclaim a few hours each week.

The Haynes Institute version organizes the work under four plain movements: plan, prepare, perform, and produce. That structure fits Beulah and Main's Household department because it is concrete enough to use on an ordinary week.

## Plan

Choose one or two reliable teaching slots and keep them modest. A Saturday morning hour or a weeknight after dinner can do real work if it repeats.

## Prepare

Read the material ahead of time. Put the books, pencils, notebooks, and table in order before the children arrive. Preparation does not need to be elaborate, but it does need to be real.

## Perform

Do the lesson. Pray, read, explain, ask questions, write a little, and repeat. The work may feel small while it is happening.

## Produce

Keep evidence of growth. Save papers, record recitations, visit museums and libraries, and make the learning visible to the children.

This is the sort of piece Beulah and Main should publish often: not merely commentary on the state of things, but a usable practice for a household.
